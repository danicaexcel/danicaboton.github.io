SPX Logistics Operations & Automated Encoding System

A first-mile logistics operations workspace that preserves the client’s familiar Daily Log and summary reporting while adding route progress, attendance, trip handover and pay, closing-time risk monitoring, owner visibility, automated escalation, and screenshot OCR intake.

Preserve the familiar report; organize the operating system around it

The client already depended on a detailed daily workbook, including store timing/closing information, route ownership, pickup counts, warehouse-trip handovers, and summary calculations. The goal is not to force a new report. The workspace keeps those concepts intact, adds role-based visibility and automation around them, and uses a normalized automation layer behind the familiar operating surface.

What needed to change

Admins continuously monitored the SPX mobile app, manually encoded seller and pickup activity into Excel, watched store operating and closing times, checked route and driver/assistant progress, followed up late drivers, recorded missed or unable-to-pickup cases, logged warehouse handover trips, maintained attendance, and manually reported status to the owner. The work was operationally important but fragmented across repetitive supervision and a human-maintained workbook.

What I designed

I organized the existing reporting process into a connected operations workspace. The detailed Daily Log remains the familiar record the client knows, while route progress, closing-time risk, attendance, trip handover, compensation, owner visibility, and audit history are derived around it. Self-hosted n8n handles scheduled monitoring, WhatsApp follow-up, escalation, status synchronization, and hourly owner reporting without creating a second disconnected operating system.

Each seller/store pickup row belongs to a route and an assigned driver or driver+assistant team. That allows the same Daily Log to report CO, PR, and TA progress without creating another task tracker.

Warehouse + pay

Trip evidence →
compensation

Handover quantities are operational and financial evidence.

The day does not end at pickup. Warehouse trips preserve the trip number, route, responsible driver, assistant, handed-over parcel count, and unload state. Those records are the basis for compensation.

Compensation rule: handed-over parcels × $0.03. If both a driver and assistant perform the trip, the amount is split 50/50. If only the driver is involved, the driver receives 100% of the trip amount.

Attendance

People status

Admins, drivers, and assistants are attendance-tracked.

Login and logout timestamps create a separate attendance record with role, elapsed time, current status, and last activity. Attendance is related to the operation but kept distinct from route progress and trip compensation so each record answers a clear business question.

Workflow 01

Driver Late Detection

Every five minutes, planned start is compared with actual start. A late driver receives a WhatsApp follow-up and the follow-up count increments.

Escalation

Maximum three attempts

After three unanswered follow-ups the driver is marked unresponsive/escalated and admins are told to intervene manually.

Workflow 02

Hourly Owner Report

Route progress, driver state, active risks, escalation status, and operational progress are aggregated into a structured owner update.

Outcome / current state

The project is represented as a shared logistics operations workspace rather than an isolated OCR utility. Admins keep the reporting structure they already know. Drivers and assistants are traceable through attendance, route work, and warehouse handovers. The owner can see route progress, last-update time, current team/trip status, and missed-pickup risk. OCR remains the controlled next-step input adapter for reducing SPX screenshot re-encoding.
